# Anchor Home Services — Design Direction

## Three possible directions

### 1. Field Manual Modernism
**Very Brief Intro:** A crisp service-operation aesthetic that borrows from professionally printed field manuals, dispatch boards, and dependable industrial equipment without becoming nostalgic. It makes everyday home-service coordination feel unusually calm and legible.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Domestic Infrastructure Gallery
**Very Brief Intro:** A quiet editorial approach where pipes, vents, airflow, and water become elevated visual objects within generous gallery-like compositions. It makes invisible household systems feel considered and valuable.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. Civic Signal
**Very Brief Intro:** A bright, information-forward identity inspired by city wayfinding and emergency communication systems. It turns urgent service decisions into a clear, reassuring sequence of signals.

**Probability:** 0.09

---

## Chosen approach: Field Manual Modernism

### Design Movement
**Field Manual Modernism** blends the typographic pragmatism of mid-century operational manuals with contemporary product-design restraint. It rejects the typical contractor-site clichés in favor of an interface that feels as organized as a well-run dispatch desk.

### Core Principles

1. **Clarity is hospitality.** Every decision, from booking urgency to service-area eligibility, gets an immediate, plainly written answer.
2. **Operational details are visual assets.** Status labels, time windows, coverage cues, and technician availability are treated as the brand’s proof of competence.
3. **Asymmetry earns attention.** Large image fields and aligned information rails create a confident, non-template composition rather than centered marketing blocks.
4. **Quiet, tactile precision.** Fine grain, sturdy outlines, measured spacing, and modest motion express reliability without decorative excess.

### Color Philosophy
The base is **porcelain white and cool mineral gray**, which keeps dense booking information breathable and avoids the warm-cream contractor aesthetic. **Anchor Cobalt** is the unmistakable signal color: a deep saturated blue that evokes dependable water, clean technical instruments, and a marked route on a field map. Moss-green confirms normal service states; a restrained oxblood red is reserved exclusively for emergencies and unavailable capacity, making urgency informative rather than theatrical.

### Layout Paradigm
The site is organized around a **dispatch rail**: a narrow vertical information lane that carries status, labels, and directional markers alongside larger content fields. On desktop, sections intentionally offset from this rail, with imagery stretching beyond the content plane and cards crossing boundaries like clipped job tickets. On mobile, the rail collapses into a compact status strip so the hierarchy remains intact without horizontal crowding.

### Signature Elements

1. **The Anchor Line:** a narrow cobalt line with a circular terminus that connects navigation, section markers, booking steps, and footer details.
2. **Job Tickets:** square-cornered, softly shadowed cards with a coded header strip, used for services, slots, plan selections, and confirmations.
3. **Field Marks:** compact uppercase labels, short rule lines, and modest grid textures that recall a durable service manual.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should reassure rather than entertain. Every selection changes a visible status summary, form validation responds in place, and the booking flow makes progress and capacity explicit. Buttons are firm controls with a slight physical press; tooltips and details appear at the point of uncertainty rather than adding explanatory clutter.

### Animation
The first page load reveals the Anchor Line, then the hero content, followed by job tickets in a staggered sequence of 45–70 ms. Hover states use short opacity and translate transitions under 220 ms; the primary button compresses subtly on press. Booking state changes use a 180–240 ms crossfade/slide that preserves the user’s visual location. All nonessential motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
**DM Sans** provides an engineered, readable interface voice across controls, metadata, and body copy. **Space Grotesk** supplies the display hierarchy with broad, slightly unconventional letterforms for key headlines and prices. Headlines use tight tracking and large scale; field labels use small uppercase DM Sans with generous letter spacing; service descriptions remain compact and conversational.

### Brand Essence
**Anchor Home Services is the dependable, dispatch-smart HVAC and plumbing partner for homeowners who want a clear answer and a scheduled expert, not a vague callback.**

**Personality:** composed, capable, straight-talking.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and action-oriented. CTAs use direct operational language. Microcopy says what will happen next, including limits and trade-offs, rather than hiding them.

> “Tell us what’s happening. We’ll route the right response.”

> “This time is held for a technician who is actually available.”

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is an abstract **anchor-pin**: a cobalt vertical route line ending in a circular location point, stabilized by two small horizontal flukes. It reads simultaneously as an anchor, a service route, and a precise map pin. The wordmark pairs a compact all-caps “ANCHOR” with a lighter, spaced “HOME SERVICES” descriptor; no default-font treatment or icon-with-text lockup should carry the brand alone.

### Signature Brand Color
**Anchor Cobalt — #1957C2.**
